Structured's rail, in DailyBrief's paper

You liked Structured's vertical timeline and the current Paper / plum aesthetic. These three keep the paper, serif day name, plum-for-now rule and the ink "Do first" card, and each borrows a different amount of Structured's mechanics: capsules sized by duration, icons on the rail, gaps written as notes, and a plus button for adding.

Borrowed from Structured

  • Rail with capsule nodes sized by duration
  • Icon per event, inside the node

What varies below

  • V1 puts the capsule on the rail, titles beside it
  • V2 keeps your cards, the capsule becomes the node

V1 · Capsule railmost Structured

The capsule is the rail. Height is duration (1 h ≈ 100 px), the icon sits in the capsule, and the gaps between events become one italic line: "coffee, 1 h", "free until 12:30".

  • "Do first" moves onto the rail as the first plum capsule at 7:15, so the day literally starts with it.
  • Three node fills: plum = now/do-first, plum tint = meeting, sand = personal; dashed = a reply slot.
  • Week strip gets event dots per day (Tuesday's orange dot is the RetaTrack shot).
Pick this if you want to feel the shape of the day at a glance, and are happy to scroll for the evening.

V2 · Card railthe balanced mix

Your current cards and the ink "Do first", but each card now hangs off a duration-sized capsule node with its icon. The rail line runs behind the nodes; the Now line stays.

  • Cards gain a duration chip ("30 m") so length is visible twice: in the node and in the text.
  • Reply slots keep the dashed treatment on both node and card.
  • Second lines get useful: "you have the figure in OpsCheck", "bring the midday check".
Pick this if you want the least change from the current design with the biggest gain in readability. My recommendation.

V3 · Planner pagetrue to the hour

A ruled paper planner, 7 AM to 7 PM, every block at its real height (46 px per hour). Nothing is compressed, so free time shows as actual empty paper with a note in it.

  • Serif hour numerals down the margin, a thin red-plum "7:05" line across the page like a bookmark ribbon.
  • The headline becomes the time itself ("7:05") since the page is about hours.
  • Short items (10–15 min) are single-line slivers; long ones get a second line and an icon.
Pick this if you want honesty about how full the day is; busy days will look busy, and that's the point.
