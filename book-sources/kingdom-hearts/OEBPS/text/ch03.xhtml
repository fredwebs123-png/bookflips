Chapter Three

Chain of Memories

Game Boy Advance · 2004

Rather than jump straight to a sequel, the series took a detour. Kingdom Hearts: Chain of Memories arrived on the Game Boy Advance in 2004 and picks up immediately after the first game. Sora, Donald and Goofy walk a lonely road searching for Riku and the King, until a hooded stranger points them toward a white, featureless building: Castle Oblivion.

“In this place, to find is to lose, and to lose is to find.”

A castle that eats memories

Inside the castle, each floor is built from Sora’s memories, recreating worlds he has already visited. But with every floor he climbs, he forgets a little more. A girl named Naminé appears in his recollections, a girl Sora is sure he has always known, and his feelings for her grow stronger even as his memories of Kairi grow fainter.

The castle is run by members of a mysterious group in black coats: Organization XIII. This is their first appearance, and several of them — Marluxia, Larxene, Axel, Vexen, Zexion and Lexaeus — scheme against each other as much as against Sora. Naminé, it turns out, has the power to rewrite memories, and she is being used to turn Sora into a pawn.

Riku’s road back

A second campaign, Reverse/Rebirth, follows Riku as he fights his way up from the castle’s depths, haunted by the darkness of Ansem that still lingers in his heart. Where Sora’s story is about losing himself, Riku’s is about learning to face what he’s done and decide to walk “the road to dawn,” between light and darkness.

Cards on the table

Chain of Memories replaced traditional combat with cards. Attacks, spells, items and summons were all cards in a deck, and players could combine them into powerful “sleights.” It was divisive at the time but has since earned real respect for its depth.

Remade in 3DA full 3D remake, Re:Chain of Memories, launched on PlayStation 2 in Japan in 2007 and in North America in 2008. It is the version most players know today, included in the Kingdom Hearts HD 1.5 ReMIX collection.

The game ends with Naminé restoring Sora’s memories at the cost of everything that happened in the castle. Sora falls into a long sleep inside a pod-like flower, set to wake — with no memory of Castle Oblivion — when it is time for Kingdom Hearts II.
