Introduction

Where Hearts Begin

Kingdom Hearts should not work. On paper it is a collision of two worlds that have almost nothing in common: the sprawling, melancholy fantasy of Square’s role-playing games and the bright, hand-drawn optimism of Disney animation. Put Cloud Strife and Winnie the Pooh in the same game and you expect a novelty, a curiosity, maybe a spin-off that is forgotten in a year.

Instead, the meeting of those two worlds became one of the most beloved series in gaming. It has sold tens of millions of copies across more than a dozen titles, filled concert halls with orchestras playing its music, and built a mythology so dense that “explaining the plot of Kingdom Hearts” has become an internet joke all of its own.

“May your heart be your guiding key.”

At its center is a boy named Sora. He lives on a small chain of islands with his best friends, Riku and Kairi, and like many kids on many islands, he dreams of what lies beyond the horizon. When darkness swallows his home, Sora finds himself holding a Keyblade — a weapon shaped like an oversized key — and traveling across worlds pulled straight from Disney films alongside Donald Duck and Goofy.

That simple setup grows, game after game, into a story about memory, identity, friendship and what it means to have a heart at all. Characters split into pieces. Pieces become people. People who should not exist ask whether they matter. And through all of it runs the series’ most stubborn idea: that the bonds between friends are a kind of light no darkness can fully put out.

How to read this book

The chapters ahead follow the games roughly in the order they were released, because that is how most fans met the story and it is the clearest way to see the saga grow. After the games, you will find chapters on the characters, the worlds, the music and the themes that tie it all together, followed by a chronological timeline and a glossary for when the terminology gets heavy. (It will get heavy.)

Spoiler warningThis companion discusses the stories of every major game, including endings and twists. If you are playing for the first time, consider reading each game’s chapter after you finish it.

Swipe or tap the edges of the page to turn. Tap the middle of the page to bring up the contents, settings and your place in the book. If you are reading on a foldable, open it up: this book was laid out for two pages side by side, the way a book should be.
