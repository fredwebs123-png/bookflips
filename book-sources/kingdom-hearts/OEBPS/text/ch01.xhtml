Chapter One

An Elevator Pitch

How Square and Disney ended up in the same game

The most famous story about the birth of Kingdom Hearts takes place in an elevator. In the late 1990s, Square’s Tokyo offices were in the same building as Disney Interactive Japan. As the story goes, producer Shinji Hashimoto struck up a conversation with a Disney executive, and the idea of a collaboration took shape from there.

It is the kind of tale that may have been polished over years of retelling, but it captures something true: Kingdom Hearts came from a chance meeting of two companies that never expected to work together.

Enter Tetsuya Nomura

The project landed with Tetsuya Nomura, a character designer best known for his work on Final Fantasy VII and VIII. Nomura has recalled overhearing a discussion about how only characters as famous as Mickey Mouse could hope to compete with the platformers of the time. That ambition — a 3D action game with characters everyone in the world already knew — became the seed of the project.

Nomura went on to direct the series, and his fingerprints are everywhere: the zipper-covered outfits, the oversized shoes, the dramatic hair, and the habit of hiding enormous story revelations in secret endings that only the most dedicated players would ever see.

Did you know?Early in development, the game was pitched as something lighter and more directly aimed at a young audience. Nomura pushed for a deeper story, reasoning that a game starring Disney characters would need more than familiar faces to satisfy fans of Square’s RPGs.

Making Disney feel like Disney

Working with Disney meant working within Disney’s rules. Characters had to move, speak and behave the way they did in their films. Worlds were built to echo their source material scene by scene, sometimes shot for shot. Donald could be grumpy, but never cruel. Goofy could be silly, but he also had to be loyal, and as players eventually learned, surprisingly wise.

At the same time, the team created a new cast to stand at the center of it all. Sora, Riku and Kairi were original characters, and so was the threat they faced: the Heartless, creatures born from the darkness in people’s hearts. Final Fantasy characters such as Leon, Cloud, Aerith, Yuffie and Sephiroth appeared as supporting players, often in reimagined roles.

A game nobody could predict

When the first game was revealed, few people knew what to make of it. Was it a kids’ game? An RPG? An action title? The answer turned out to be all of these at once, and that refusal to fit in one box became the series’ signature. Two decades later, it still does not fit neatly anywhere — which is exactly why people love it.
