Chapter Thirteen

Simple and Clean

The music of Kingdom Hearts

Ask fans what they love most about Kingdom Hearts, and many will answer before you finish the question: the music. The series’ soundtracks are led by composer Yoko Shimomura, whose melodies range from bouncy and playful to sweeping and sorrowful, sometimes within the same track.

Dearly Beloved

Every main game opens with a new arrangement of “Dearly Beloved,” a gentle piano theme that plays on the title screen. It has become the series’ signature — a few notes that instantly bring back memories for millions of players. Other favorites include “Destati,” the choral theme of the Dive to the Heart, the warm “Hand in Hand,” the moody “Traverse Town,” and “Vector to the Heavens” from 358/2 Days.

A handful of piano notes, and you’re back on the islands.

Utada Hikaru

The series’ theme songs are sung by Utada Hikaru, one of Japan’s best-selling artists. Each main game has a song in Japanese and in English:

  • Kingdom Hearts — “Hikari” / “Simple and Clean”
  • Kingdom Hearts II — “Passion” / “Sanctuary”
  • Kingdom Hearts III — “Chikai” / “Don’t Think Twice,” with “Face My Fears,” a collaboration with Skrillex, as the opening theme

“Simple and Clean” in particular has outlived its era. It shows up in remixes, covers and concert encores, and its closing words — about taking a chance on someone — feel like the series in miniature.

In concert

The music has been celebrated in live orchestral concerts around the world, including the Kingdom Hearts Orchestra World Tour, where full orchestras played the scores alongside footage from the games. The rhythm game Melody of Memory turned that legacy into a playable greatest-hits collection.

A team effortWhile Shimomura leads, later games feature contributions from other composers, including Takeharu Ishimoto and Tsuyoshi Sekito, helping to fill out ever-larger soundtracks.
