Chapter Two

Kingdom Hearts

PlayStation 2 · 2002

The first Kingdom Hearts arrived on the PlayStation 2 in Japan in March 2002 and in North America that September. It opens not on a battlefield but inside a dream: Sora falling through dark water, landing on a stained-glass platform, and being asked by an unseen voice what he values most.

That opening, known to fans as the Dive to the Heart, sets the tone. This is a game about the inner life of its characters as much as the worlds they visit.

The story

On Destiny Islands, Sora, Riku and Kairi build a raft to sail away and see other worlds. The night before they leave, a storm of darkness consumes the islands. Riku reaches willingly into the dark. Kairi vanishes. And Sora is left holding the Keyblade, a weapon that the Heartless fear because it can unlock — and lock — the hearts of worlds.

Elsewhere, King Mickey has left his castle, leaving behind a letter asking his court magician Donald and his captain of the guard Goofy to find “the key.” When they cross paths with Sora in Traverse Town, the trio is formed: Sora searching for his friends, Donald and Goofy searching for their king.

Their journey takes them through Wonderland, Agrabah, Halloween Town, Atlantica, Neverland and more, sealing each world’s keyhole before the Heartless can devour its heart. Meanwhile, Riku falls in with the villainous Maleficent, who promises to help him find Kairi, and the two friends become rivals.

“My friends are my power!”

At Hollow Bastion, the truth arrives in pieces. Kairi’s heart has been hiding inside Sora all along. To free it, Sora turns a Keyblade made from captured hearts on himself, becoming a Heartless for a moment — until Kairi’s embrace brings him back. Riku, meanwhile, has been possessed by a figure calling himself Ansem, the Seeker of Darkness.

The final battle at the End of the World ends with Sora and Mickey sealing the Door to Darkness from both sides, with Riku and the King trapped on the far side. Sora and Kairi are pulled apart as the worlds restore themselves. Her last words to him are a promise: she’ll always be with him.

How it played

Kingdom Hearts was a real-time action RPG. Sora attacked with combos, cast magic like Fire and Blizzard, called on Disney summons such as Simba and Genie, and fought alongside AI-controlled Donald and Goofy. Between worlds, players piloted a customizable Gummi Ship in short shooting sequences.

The secret endingPlayers who completed enough of the game unlocked a short secret ending that hinted at much more to come. The Japanese-only Final Mix edition released later in 2002 went further, adding new content and a cryptic teaser called “Another Side, Another Story” that set the stage for the sequels.

It was rough around the edges — the camera could be a battle of its own — but nothing else felt like it. It was cozy and strange, funny and sad, and it ended with a promise of more.
