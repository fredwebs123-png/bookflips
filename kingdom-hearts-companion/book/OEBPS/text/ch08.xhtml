Chapter Eight

The Age of Fairy Tales

χ, Union χ, Back Cover and Dark Road

Not every part of the story was told on consoles. Kingdom Hearts χ (pronounced “chi”) launched in 2013 as a Japanese browser game and later grew into a worldwide mobile game, eventually known as Kingdom Hearts Union χ[Cross]. It reaches back further than any other entry, to a time long before Sora: the Age of Fairy Tales.

Daybreak Town and the Foretellers

In this ancient era, countless Keyblade wielders live in Daybreak Town and gather Lux, a form of light, by fighting Heartless. They belong to five Unions, each led by a Foreteller: Ira, Invi, Aced, Ava and Gula. All five were apprentices of a hooded figure called the Master of Masters, who gave each of them a copy of a Book of Prophecies and a secret task.

The Master of Masters also gave a sixth apprentice, Luxu, a unique mission and a Keyblade with a strange eye on its side. That Keyblade, passed down through the ages, would one day reach the hands of Master Xehanort. Luxu himself is quietly woven into the later games, which makes him one of the series’ most intriguing mysteries.

The Foretellers’ distrust ends in the Keyblade War — the battle that shattered the world.

Back Cover

The Foretellers’ story is told from their point of view in Kingdom Hearts χ Back Cover, an hour-long animated film included in the HD 2.8 Final Chapter Prologue collection in 2017. It is one of the best ways to understand the ancient lore without playing a mobile game that is no longer online.

Union χ and Dark Road

Union χ continued the story with a group of young wielders, including Ephemer, Skuld, Brain, Lauriam and Strelitzia, who survive the Keyblade War and try to rebuild. Lauriam later became Marluxia — and Strelitzia, his sister, would reappear in the reveal of Kingdom Hearts IV.

Kingdom Hearts Dark Road, launched in 2020 within the same app, told the story of a young Xehanort and his friend Eraqus as students in the city of Scala ad Caelum. Online service for Union χ ended in 2021, and the app was reworked into an offline version that preserves its story.

Why it mattersMany of the ideas driving Kingdom Hearts III’s ending — the Book of Prophecies, the Master of Masters, the “Lost Masters” — come from χ. You don’t need to play it, but knowing it makes the later games click.
